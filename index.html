Gas exchange

At the lung’s air–blood barrier, oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse at the same time in opposite directions.

  • O₂ diffuses from alveolar air into plasma, then enters a red blood cell and binds to hemoglobin.
  • CO₂ diffuses from the blood into alveolar air and leaves when you exhale.

Each gas moves down its own partial-pressure gradient. Air and blood stay separate.

How blood carries carbon dioxide

In tissues, CO₂ enters red blood cells. Much of it becomes bicarbonate and travels in plasma. In the lungs, bicarbonate reenters red cells and becomes CO₂ again. Some CO₂ also travels dissolved or attached to hemoglobin.

Why does this matter?

Your cells and enzymes work best when conditions inside your body stay within a healthy range. By helping regulate carbon dioxide, your respiratory system also helps maintain blood pH.

Why does CO₂ change pH?

Some dissolved CO₂ reacts with water to form carbonic acid. It can release hydrogen ions, which lower pH. When you exhale more CO₂, that reaction shifts and blood pH can rise toward its starting level.

Three essential functions

Three reasons you can’t ignore it.

Every breath powers more than you think.

Fuel your cells.

Oxygen enters your blood through your lungs. Your blood carries it to cells, where it helps release energy from food during cellular respiration.

Ask your teacher before submitting the website as your project. If you record the tour as a video commercial, appear on camera as required by the assignment.

  1. The opening

    Meet your respiratory system. Every breath brings in oxygen, clears carbon dioxide, and helps keep your body in balance.

  2. Follow the airways

    Air moves down the trachea and into two main bronchi. Inside each lung, the airways branch into smaller and smaller passages.

  3. A breath in motion

    Watch the diaphragm move down. Your chest expands, pressure drops, and air flows in. As the diaphragm relaxes, the lungs recoil and air flows out.

  4. The microscopic exchange

    At these tiny air sacs, oxygen diffuses into your blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses in the opposite direction so you can breathe it out.

  5. How breathing keeps balance

    When extra carbon dioxide builds up, blood becomes more acidic. The brain increases breathing, which removes more carbon dioxide and brings pH back toward its starting level. Stable pH helps cells and enzymes work.

  6. Three essential functions

    It delivers oxygen to your cells, removes waste carbon dioxide, and helps regulate blood pH. All three jobs happen with every breath.
