O₂

Oxygen intake

Fuel your cells.

Oxygen moves from tiny air sacs in your lungs into your blood. Your cells use this oxygen to get energy from food.

CO₂

Carbon dioxide removal

Take out the waste.

Cells make carbon dioxide as they release energy from food. Blood carries it to your lungs, and you breathe it out.

pH

Blood pH regulation

Keep the balance.

When more carbon dioxide builds up, blood gets more acidic. Breathing out extra carbon dioxide helps bring blood pH back toward normal.

Homeostasis in action

How the respiratory system keeps blood pH steady

  1. CO₂ rises

    When CO₂ in the blood increases, the blood becomes more acidic and pH decreases.

  2. Breathing adjusts

    Sensors notice the change. Your brain tells you to breathe faster or deeper.

  3. More CO₂ leaves

    Faster or deeper breathing removes more CO₂.

  4. pH recovers

    As the CO₂ level decreases, blood pH increases toward normal.

Why it matters. Your cells use enzymes to break down food. These enzymes work best in a certain pH range. Breathing helps keep blood pH in that range.
